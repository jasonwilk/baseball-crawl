## What this file is, and why it exists separately

E-276's `epic.md` grew to ~1,300 lines, and its History carried the session's process findings with their instances and derivations. Those findings are the epic's most transferable output **and an implementer never needs them**, so they are extracted here and the History keeps a one-line index plus this pointer.

**The selector used**, and it is the general test for anything else in that file: *is this needed at the point of implementing, or is it a record of how we arrived at the decision?* The first belongs in a story or a Technical Note; the second belongs here.

**What deliberately did NOT move, and must not be swept here later**: the design, the preconditions, the ACs' basis, the forbidden shapes, the five required inputs, the operator-facing scope section, the régime tables (though not their derivations), and the load-bearing prose corrections. **Extraction method**: entries are preserved **verbatim and in their original order**, not re-cut thematically. Several of these findings turn on single words (`bound` vs `rate`, `safety` vs `availability`, `self-healing` vs `permanent-while-broken`), and a re-authored summary of a finding about summaries carrying truth-values past their qualification would be the fifth host applied to the extraction itself. The thematic grouping lives in the index below, not in the text.

**Chronological epic-status entries stayed in `epic.md`** — creation, the gate-semantics settlement, the numbering decision, and the round that retracted the roster-lock fix-neutrality claim. Those are epic history proper, not process mechanisms.

## The findings, verbatim

- **THE SIGNATURE DEFECT FINALLY HAS A CHECKABLE FORM** [SE, offered as mechanical rather than as a call for care]:

  > **What region did I sample, and what region am I claiming?**

  Every instance today was *a correct computation carried past its case*, and that framing diagnoses but does not prevent — you cannot instruct someone to notice a boundary they have not seen. **This question has a natural checkpoint even though the inferential step does not**: it is answerable at the moment of writing the claim, from the claim and the run alone, without re-deriving anything.

  The instance that produced it: a strand run sampled `R=13, c=14` — **the first row of the LOCK régime** — and reported the trade for the *whole* widening region from that single point. **Confirming a claim quantified over a region requires sampling the region, not a point inside it.** It is the operational half of CR's *"a confirmation is only as independent as its most-constrained axis"*, and the two belong together: one asks which axes you varied, this asks over what range.

- **A THIRD INSTANCE OF A CORRECT VERDICT RESTING ON A PREMISE THAT DOES NOT SUPPORT IT — and the three together make it this epic's most reliable failure.** §4 used **rows**-re-derivability to license a claim about **deletes**; §6 used re-derivability to license adequacy in the one input where re-derivability is false; §3 offered **change-control on a constant** (the cap cannot move silently — true, and verified) as evidence for **adequacy of the guard at its current value** — which is false at the shipped value by the epic's own executed evidence, since a degrading crawl erodes 13 rows to 1 **at cap 2, with nobody tuning anything.**

  **All three verdicts were RIGHT. All three reasons were wrong.** `.claude/rules/tool-output-integrity.md` already names why they survive — *a verdict's stated reason rots independently of the verdict, and a correct conclusion immunizes its false premise* — so **any check asking "was the call right?" passes all three.** The only check that catches them is reopening the premise, which is what the adversarial reads did.

  **⚠️ A FOURTH INSTANCE, and it is a DISTINCT SUB-SHAPE with a different detection question** [CR-2]. The first three are *correct verdict on an unsupporting premise*. The fourth is **correct FACT on the WRONG AXIS**: the §3 grading cited *"the cap is pinned, it cannot move silently"* — **true, verifiable, about the same constant** — as evidence of the cap's **adequacy at its current value**. The two are **orthogonal**: change control triggers only on mutation, the adequacy failure requires none. They never touch.

  **The detection question differs, which is why it needs its own name.** For the first three you ask *does the premise support the conclusion?* For this one you ask **does the fact bear on the claim at all?** A premise that is merely insufficient still points at the right subject; a fact on the wrong axis does not, and **checking it harder confirms it harder.**

  **Orthogonal is WORSE than opposed, because tension is visible and orthogonality is not** — and it *manufactures* confidence rather than merely failing to remove it.

  **In each case the correct reason was already on disk, one or two sections away.** For §3 it is the accepted-residual argument: the operator ruled V1 knowing the trade, and the rate residual is recorded as *"ACCEPTED, not closed"* because the cap cannot distinguish a genuine two-a-week departure sequence from slow degradation — they are byte-identical, and no gate could separate them on the evidence a crawl carries. **The finding is non-blocking because the residual was priced and taken, not because a test pins a constant.** Each author reached for the nearer fact rather than the right one.

- **THE CORRECTION OF A DEFECT IS NOT A SAFER PLACE THAN THE DEFECT WAS.** Recorded as its own finding rather than a footnote, because it is the strongest argument in this record for adversarial re-execution over review-by-reading.

  > **PM over-claimed, then over-retracted, and neither was caught by its author.**

  The over-claim was a confounded number inflating a methodology finding about PM's own method failure. The over-correction was an honest retraction about *one run* that sat **adjacent to the hazard description**, where it read as deflating *the hazard* — **the identical position, one step later.** And it was committed *while writing up the correction of a different defect*.

  **A self-review of a correction is exactly where confidence peaks**, which is why the correction inherits the original's blind spot rather than escaping it. The tell is the same one this epic records elsewhere from the other side: **the two defects the reviewing layer caught in PM were both cases where PM was most confident it had just been careful.**

  **Consequence for the record**: a defect and its fix are **two claims, not one claim and its resolution** — and the fix gets less scrutiny precisely because the surrounding attention has just been spent. Neither this instance nor its predecessor yields to "check it again"; both required a reader who had not written either.

- **A DISTINCT SPECIES: VERIFICATION THAT INHERITS THE THING IT WAS MEANT TO CATCH.** An exhaustive sweep was run *to verify another agent's claim* and **reproduced that agent's blind spot** — both had held the churn axis constant, independently. SE's own words: ***"I did not verify your claim. I reproduced your blind spot with different arithmetic."*** Two agents, one fixed axis, and **the agreement made the claim more credible than the original rather than testing it.** Found by a third varying the axis the other two had fixed.

  **Not "an unchecked claim" and not "a correct computation carried past its case"** — it is a *check* that failed in the same way as the thing it checked, which is why it made matters worse rather than merely failing to help. **Operational consequence, and it is what makes this recordable rather than merely cautionary: a confirmation is only as independent as its MOST-CONSTRAINED AXIS — so a verification must state which axes it VARIED, not just its result.** "Confirmed independently" is not a claim anyone can evaluate; "swept over A, B, C with D fixed" is.

- **FOUR PARTICIPANTS CORRECTED A CREDIT DOWNWARD TODAY, and this is recorded for a structural reason rather than as courtesy.** A reviewer declined attribution for a residue finding because its own corollary had asserted a property of `merge_player_pair` **it had not read** — *"the same error as yours in the same paragraph, and I said it with more confidence."* Another credited a colleague's independent run that its own report had absorbed. **An accurate attribution record is what makes the failure tally usable**: a tally inflated by generous crediting would mislead exactly the reader it is written for, because the interesting quantity is not who erred but *which check caught what*, and a misattributed catch teaches the wrong check.

- **THE COMPLETED TALLY, which supersedes every count of "findings" in this record** [DE's final synthesis]:

  **Seventeen defects, zero of them arithmetic.** That is the epic's subject measured on itself, and it is why "check the maths" would have caught none of them.

  **And it explains why BOTH review passes were needed rather than one** [DE]:

  > An author checking their own computation **re-derives the computation, which is correct, and never re-examines the scope claim, which is the part that was wrong.**

  **Record this as COMPLEMENTARY to the earlier entry finding the self-audit the higher-yield pass — not as a correction of it.** Both are true and they cover different failure modes: the self-audit catches what only its author can see (a frame they know they chose), and cross-review catches what its author structurally cannot (a scope claim invisible from inside the case that produced it). - **THE SHARPEST PROCESS FINDING OF THE SESSION, and it is about a WORD CLASS: three findings needed a qualifier corrected in the direction of sounding SAFER — *"self-healing"*, *"≤2 as a total"*, and *"bounded"* versus *"rate"*.** Each was caught by a different agent, each about someone else's phrasing, and **none by its author.**

  ### ⚠️ AND THE MIRROR, DEMONSTRATED RATHER THAN HYPOTHESISED — read this entry and the one above as ONE finding

  **The author of the entry above then committed its inverse, twice, in the same document.** PM's two errors this session both ran toward sounding **more alarming**: an inverted polarity attaching *"safety"* to a fail-CLOSED asymmetry, and an operator-facing summary that stated the régime where the shipped design corrupts a stat while **omitting the régime where today's code permanently locks the roster and the design fixes it.** Both were committed while consciously guarding against the reassuring-qualifier failure.

  **The consequence is what makes the pair usable: a rule against soft qualifiers that produces hard ones has not fixed anything — it has MOVED the bias.**

The ASYMMETRY is.** That resolves what would otherwise read as a contradiction in the record — `tool-output-integrity.md` saying alarming prose is self-protecting, the entry above saying reassuring prose is too.   **The check is therefore NOT "does the wording sound appropriately grave."** It is: **are BOTH sides of the trade stated?** A widening reported without its alternative is not a trade, it is a defect — and the same sentence with the alternative attached is a decision the reader can actually make.

  **Both instances were caught from outside**, and the second landed inside *"the section whose whole purpose is to let an operator judge fairly."* Same structure as everything else in this record: the defect lands in the artifact designed to prevent it, is invisible to its author, and is caught by a reader who did not write it. **That is also the argument for why this pair, and not the mechanism generalisation, is the codification candidate — it names a check that a non-author can run in one pass.**

  > **Author-owned claims are simultaneously the most reliable and the least checked — so a single error in one propagates further than an error anywhere else.**
