## 3. Build order (baseball-coach re-ranking, 2026-07-12)

**MUST (changes what a coach does Friday night / Tuesday practice):**
1. **Opposing-staff NSAA eligibility board + week-ahead arm calendar** — deterministic rules computation (engine already exists for our own staff), no sample-size caveat. "Their ace threw 85 Wednesday → not legal Friday → expect their #3." **Do not stop at eligibility — RANK the probable starter** (rest × start-history × recent cadence) and JOIN it to the blueprint (MUST #2): the game plan must be conditioned on the arm actually pitching, not the staff average (see §8 — the 2026-07-12 miss was a season-average blueprint that a starter-conditioned one would have sharpened).
2. **Loss forensics + blueprint synthesis** — per-loss autopsy (winner scored first? BB drawn? SB taken? which pitcher hit, when pulled) → "3 things teams that beat them did" (n losses always shown). Game-level counting = robust at n=5–8.
3. **Freebie ledger (self-scout)** — BB+HBP+E+SB+WP given vs taken, per game, trended. Changes practice plans; full-season sample; every kid understands it.

**SHOULD (with hard conditions):**
4. Steal traffic light per battery — grey/no-data below **5 attempts**; always show raw count ("3-for-5 caught"). Coach-facing only in named form (ethics, §5).
5. Fielder-on-out direction cards → **defensive-alignment synthesis** — binary pull/oppo, NOT zones; floor **15 BIP**. **Correction (2026-07-13, verified): buildable TODAY — `spray_charts` already persists `fielder_position` + x/y per batted ball, so pull/side AND infield-vs-outfield are computable now WITHOUT `final_details`** (`final_details` would only ADD contact-quality, not gate this). And don't stop at "he pulls" — emit the ALIGNMENT: `GB% + side → "shade 3B/SS to the hole, guard the 5-6 gap" | "infield in" | "OF deep"`. Pairs with the leg-hit ledger below (a pull-GB hitter who also legs out infield singles → 3B plays in AND toward the line). See §8.
6. Bench matchup advisor — **the biggest gap the brainstorm missed**: pinch-hit/matchup helper for OUR bench vs THEIR reliever (handedness-gated). Highest-leverage in-game call with no tool.
7. Coach-tendency scouting — does THEIR COACH bunt runner-on-1st-none-out in tight games; pitch-around/IBB patterns for their 3-4 hitters; late-inning defensive-sub patterns. Coach behavior ≠ player tendency.
8. Productive-out/execution rate — flip side of the freebie ledger (are the things we TRY working).
9. Lineup prediction from batting-order history.

**DEMOTED:** line-score game-flow profile ("fast starters", comeback record) = context, not a decision — Tier 2 at best. Per-arm approach word ("GRIND"/"AMBUSH") = presentation layer over FPS%, not a build item. RISP/clutch splits = near-unusable at HS PA counts; never a headline stat.

**Sample-size doctrine:** eligibility = rules, no caveat. Game-level counting across a season = solid. PA-level rates = "leans, not truths," with floors (20 PA batting / 15 IP pitching per coach memory; 15 BIP directional; 5 attempts steal light) and grey states enforced upstream, never prose-hedged only. FPS%/pitch data = charted-games subset; always badge the real denominator (trust-surface philosophy, extends 2026-04-12 signal).

**Consumption verdicts (15–18yo):** YES: traffic-light iconography (+grey state), plain-language instruction voice ("be ready to swing at strike one," never "FPS% 71"), one-sentence opponent baseball cards, 60-second locker-room script read by the coach. NO: player-facing pregame quiz ("adult classroom fantasy" — fold content into the script), post-game accountability card is staff/captains-only. **Operational constraint: dugout device restrictions → player artifacts must survive as laminated print.**

**Ethics split (hard rule):** coach-facing materials carry full names + full data. Player-facing materials describe team/situational tendencies only — never a named opposing kid next to a weakness ("they run a lot early," not "#7 can't throw"). Positioning cards may reference hitters by number for alignment only, no derogatory framing. Named steal-light is the sharpest risk (screenshot-able targeting of a 15-year-old catcher's arm).

## 5. Matchup parameterization (the operator's question: "would you have to know which team was mine?")

Pure opponent sections (eligibility, loss forensics, spray, lineup prediction) are team-agnostic — same as today's `bb report generate <public_id>`. But the highest-leverage sections are **pairings**: steal lights = OUR runners × their battery; bench advisor = OUR bench × their pen; kryptonite = their losers' profile × OUR profile; eligibility board is dated (their week's schedule × OUR game date). Design: `deep-scout <their_public_id> --vs <our_team> --date <game_date>`, degrading gracefully to opponent-only when `--vs` is absent. Our side costs nothing — our program team data is already the richest in the DB, and morning-run already knows the pairing + date, so the scheduled path gets matchup context for free.

## 6. Prompt architecture (for the LLM-synthesized sections)

Principles: (1) **compute, don't prompt** — every number is SQL-computed with `n` attached; the LLM never sees raw plays, only a fact sheet; (2) floors/grey states enforced upstream in the fact sheet, not by prose hedging; (3) **two-pass**: coach memo synthesis, then player-materials translation with the ethics rails; (4) word budgets force actionability; (5) **matchup-conditioning + range-not-point** (added post-§8 validation): the fact sheet must carry a `probable_starter` block and every staff pitching stat must be **innings-weighted** (a staff BB/7 is dominated by low-inning wild relievers and mis-estimates the arm that throws 5+ innings), and the synthesis prompt must condition the blueprint on that probable starter. Where a starter's single-game command is high-variance (his season strike% is the tell), the memo gives **both branches** — "if he's wild (his season norm) → take walks; if he locates → he doesn't miss bats, put it in play and run" — because eligibility (who *can* pitch) is deterministic but that arm's command *on the day* is not. Rates are presented as ranges with both tails planned for, never a single point stated as near-fact. Fact-sheet contract: JSON, every fact = `{value, n, status: ok|thin|no_data}` + a `vs` block when matchup context exists. Prompt templates live in the session transcript / future epic spec; core rules: use only fact-sheet numbers; cite n inline; thin → "lean" language; no_data → say so; ban "always/never"; fixed sections with budgets (Blueprint ≤3 bullets, lineup ≤9 lines, ≤600 words). Player pass: instruction voice only; drop anything the memo hedged; no named opposing-player weaknesses; laminate-ready One Card (≤5 lines) + 3 position-group cards.

## 8. Live validation — 2026-07-12, our team (us) vs Opponent A

**FINAL: our team 8, Opponent A 5** (DB-verified after regenerating Opponent A' season to load the completed game). First real end-to-end run of Prompt A against a live opponent, graded against the finished game. Verdict: **the deterministic layer is the product and it was excellent; the single miss was entirely in synthesis** — the memo led with a season-average thread that a starter-conditioned, innings-weighted, two-branch synthesis would have sharpened. Every fix below is a synthesis/fact-sheet change, not new data.

### Held up — keep as-is
- **Eligibility board (the crown jewel).** Predicted both workhorses unavailable (P10 85p 7/11 → needs 3 days; P3 88p 7/10 → needs 3) and a rested secondary arm starting. P2 (flagged ELIGIBLE, last outing 7/6) started; P3 was present only as a courtesy runner (not pitching); P10 never appeared. Deterministic, zero sample caveat — exactly the Tier-1 value.
- **Per-hitter engine above floor.** P1 (n=102 PA, 81 BIP: "elite contact, ~0 K, hard grounders pull-side, their best bat, defend the left side") went **4-for-4, 2B, 0 K**, balls to SS/LF/CF. The PA-floor approach delivers when the floor is cleared.
- **Steal light.** Green call (season 90/103 = 87% vs their battery; P1 the lone real arm) → our team ran **3-for-4**; the only caught-stealing was P1's. Keep.
- **Lineup spine.** Play-sequence reconstruction nailed **1-P9, 3-P1, 4-P5, 9-P11** exactly (DB-confirmed order), off a scouting-loaded boxscore where `batting_order` is NULL.
- **Self-scout warning proved out.** "DON'T BEAT OURSELVES" → our starter walked 2 + hit 2 in the 1st inning, gifting Opponent A both early runs and keeping a beatable team ahead 5-4 into the 6th.

### The instructive miss — synthesis led with a season-average thread
The memo's headline blueprint was "make them throw strikes / take your walks" (staff BB/7 = 7.0; winner drew 4+ BB in 15/15 losses). P2 walked only 2 in 5.1 IP; our team won not on walks but by **hitting** him (8 H), **running** (3 SB), and chasing him to a wilder reliever (P9) in the 6th. Two distinct root causes — one fixable, one not:

- **COULD have seen (method fix):** the blueprint was computed staff-aggregate, and the who's-pitching section was computed separately — **they were never joined.** Conditioned on the probable starter, P2's own line is *both* wild **and** low-whiff: **8.4 BB/7, 5.6 SO/7, 53% strikes (n=15 IP pre-game)** — worse control than the staff average, and he doesn't miss bats. The correct starter-specific plan is **two-branch**: "if he's wild (his norm) → take walks; if he locates → he can't miss bats, so put it in play and run." That second branch is exactly what won the game, and every number for it was already in the DB. The staff BB/7 aggregate also mis-led because it is dominated by low-inning wild relievers (P4 6 BB/1 IP) — **innings-weighting** would have surfaced the strike-thrower risk.
- **COULDN'T have known (irreducible):** that P2 would post a **+13-point strike-rate outlier** this afternoon (66% vs his 53% norm). Single-game pitcher command is high-variance; *who can pitch* is deterministic, but *that arm's command on the day* is not. The memo presented "they walk 5.7/gm" as near-fact; it should carry the season rate **as a range** and plan for both tails.

### Secondary lessons
- **Lineup slots drift — weight recency.** Whole-season batting-order modes put P3 (11 games at leadoff) and P6 in the starting 9; both sat (P3, a mandated-rest pitcher, only courtesy-ran). Weight the last 5–7 games, attach a per-slot confidence, and flag that a rest-day pitcher may be absent from the lineup.
- **Pitching facts belong to arms, not staffs.** Innings-weight every staff pitching stat and always print the probable starter's own line (BB/7, SO/7, strike%, hittability) beside the aggregate.
- **Self-scout should be per-pitcher for OUR probable starter.** The freebie ledger predicted the failure mode in aggregate; a per-pitcher first-inning-control line for our listed starter would have named the specific risk (a wobble-prone arm) before the game.

### Net
On the two things that most drive a game plan — *who is pitching* and *which bat must not beat you (P1)* — the report was essentially exact. The upgrade is entirely synthesis: **join the eligibility/probable-starter board to the loss-forensics blueprint, innings-weight the pitching facts, and present the probable starter's tendencies as a two-branch, range-not-point plan.** These are folded into §3 MUST #1 and §6 principle (5) above, and into the evolved Prompt A (kept with the session transcript).

### 8b. Two operator-surfaced capabilities (2026-07-13) — buildable today, no new crawling

Operator flagged two syntheses the first run under-delivered. Both were computed live on the Opponent A data already in the DB (`spray_charts` + `player_game_batting`), proving they need no new endpoints:

**(i) Leg-hit / speed-inflation ledger.** A fast hitter's batting line is padded with ground balls to 5/6 he legs out — those are speed, not hitting, and they demand a different defense (play in, quick release) while deflating his AVG. Detect via `spray_charts`: a `play_result='single'` whose `fielder_position` is an infielder (`P/C/1B/2B/3B/SS`) is an infield hit; `LF/CF/RF` is a clean hit. Report per hitter: SB, AVG, `infield-single%`, and a **SPEED-INFLATED flag** when a high-SB hitter's infield-single share is high. Live proof: **P8 .333 with 42% of singles legged out (5/12)** — his real bat is well under the line; **P6 13 SB / .156** (pure speed); **P3 18 SB, 33% legged** (top run threat); P1 .588 / 27% legged = genuinely rakes (30 singles + XBH), not a mirage. Heuristic caveat: an IF-fielded single ≈ infield hit (a ball past the bag is credited to the OF), sound but not perfect; bunt singles to C/P count. This is a NEW capability — §4-C had "speed threats" but not leg-hit de-inflation; add it to the coach memo's lineup block and the fact sheet.

**(ii) Positioning is a directive, not a description.** The first run said "P1 pulls grounders left" but never gave the alignment. Turn `GB% + side` into an instruction: **PULL+GB-left → shade 3B/SS to the hole, guard the 5-6 gap; ≥50% GB → infield honest/in; ≥45% FB → OF deep.** Live proof: P1 / P5 / P4 / P7 all read PULL+GB-left (shade left, guard the gap); P8 67% GB → in; P9 / P3 fly-ball → OF deep. Where OUR defensive personnel is known (e.g. a strong-armed 3B), the directive sharpens ("guard the line, squeeze the gap"). Per §3 SHOULD #5 this is buildable now — the doc previously mis-gated it on `final_details`.

Doctrine unchanged by these: floors still apply (15 BIP directional, small infield-single denominators shown raw, e.g. "50%, n=2" for P11 = a lean, not a fact); coach-facing may name hitters for alignment/number, player-facing stays team-tendency only (ethics split §5).

### 8c. Running-game CONCENTRATION + the "slasher" overlay (2026-07-13, operator-surfaced)

The steal light (§3 SHOULD #4) measured the opponent's aggregate success vs a battery. Operator flagged a second, orthogonal fact: **is the running game CONCENTRATED in one or two players or spread across the roster?** — because the counter differs.

- **Metric:** top-2 SB share of the team total + the #2→#3 gap. **Concentrated** (top-2 ≳65% with a clear gap) → the game plan names and neutralizes *those* runners (hold close, slide-step, pitch out, throw over) — shut down 2 guys, shut down their running game. **Distributed** (top-2 ≲45%, no gap) → a team-wide green light no single hold-play fixes; you need battery-level run control. Live contrast: **Opponent A = distributed** (121 SB / 15 runners; top-2 P3 18 + P1 15 = **27%**, no gap) — fits their all-out 87%-success profile vs weak batteries; the operator's other team (Henry 11 / Brody 5, rest near zero) = **concentrated** — key on two.
- **"Slasher" overlay:** cross the SB leaderboard with the leg-hit ledger (8b-i) and the alignment card (8b-ii). A primary base-stealer who is ALSO heavy-GB-left / high-infield-hit is the compound threat — he slaps it to 5/6, legs it out, then steals second. Defense stacks: infield **in AND shaded left**, and hold him tight. (The operator's "Brody" is the textbook case; on Opponent A the nearest analog is P4 — 14 SB, 65% GB, pulls left — though the distributed profile means no single key-on.)

**Tool-integrity note (process):** the concentration numbers were first produced by a heredoc query that came back **garbled** (58 SB / 6 runners, wrong leaderboard); a cross-check query gave the true 121 / 15 / 27%. Per `tool-output-integrity.md`, the fact sheet's SQL must be the single source and any surprising aggregate cross-checked before it reaches the memo — a scout memo built on a garbled count is worse than none.

### 8d. Battery-control card: catcher throwouts, BACKPICKS, pitcher pickoffs (2026-07-13, operator-surfaced)

Operator asked for catcher throwout rates and pickoffs by pitcher/catcher — specifically **identifying strong backpicking catchers** (a catcher who throws behind the runner to a base). All of it parses from `play_events.raw_template` already stored — zero new crawling. Verified live on Opponent C (42) and Opponent B (192).

- **Parse forms** (actor named after the base): `caught stealing {base}, catcher ${C}` = CS credited to catcher C; `picked off at {base}, catcher ${C} to {fielder}` = **BACKPICK by catcher C**; `picked off at {base}, pitcher ${P}` = pitcher pickoff; `picked off at {base}, {infielder}` = snap play; `Pickoff attempt at {base}` = a throw-over (no out); `wild pitch` / `passed ball` = battery leak.
- **Signals the card emits, per team (as the DEFENSE):** (a) **pickoff-attempt frequency/gm + base split** = how actively they hold runners (Opponent C **2.8/gm, mostly 1st** = aggressive hold; Opponent B 1.6/gm); (b) **CS%** = catcher arm on steals; (c) **backpick putouts per named catcher** = the rare weapon — flag a "STRONG BACKPICK ARM" at even 2+ (sparse event, low floor, show raw counts NOT a rate); (d) **pitcher pickoffs per arm** (Opponent B's P12 = 3 — an arm that holds); (e) **WP+PB leak rate**. Live proof: Opponent C's **P15 backpicked a runner at 3rd** (parsed and attributed correctly).
- **Coaching use — the backpick flag is DUAL-USE (threat AND weapon):**
  - *Defense-for-us (our runners):* against a backpicking catcher / aggressive hold, shorten the secondary lead, get back hard, respect the backdoor throw at first.
  - *Offense-for-us (bait the aggression):* a backpick-happy catcher can be **suckered in a first-and-third**. Runners on the corners, make the runner at first look ready to go; when the catcher throws behind to first (the backpick he loves), the runner at third breaks home. It's the catcher-tendency analog of a balk-steal — you provoke the aggressive act and cash the run. So when the card flags a STRONG BACKPICK ARM, the memo should surface the **first-and-third bait play** as OUR EDGE, not just a warning.
  - *Refinement (v2, situational):* the sharpest version detects backpicks that occurred **with a runner also on third** (the exact bait state) — a catcher who backpicks to first even then is prime to be run on. Requires reconstructing base-state from the baserunner-event sequence (no dedicated "runners-on" column); flagged as a deeper parse, not needed for v1 (the raw backpick tendency already justifies trying the play).
  - Pairs with the §4-D our-runners matchup card, the §4-D "1st-and-3rd defense book" (this is its offensive mirror), and the steal light (§3 SHOULD #4): a leaky battery (high WP+PB, low CS%) = green light; an aggressive-hold/backpick battery = tighten our leads AND bait the first-and-third.
- **Attribution rule (hard):** roll up by `player_id`, **NEVER by name.** "our runner" exists as separate roster rows on THREE teams (Opponent C 42, our program our team Freshman 106, another local team 121) plus cross-perspective duplicates — a name-keyed rollup silently merges three different players (and the cross-perspective dupes double-count one). Same duplicate-identity family as §-cross-perspective dedup / E-261; the battery card must key strictly on the UUID in the template. **Fidelity follow-up:** the operator-cited "catcher backpicked our runner at first, last game" was NOT locatable in the current DB under the scanned perspectives — parser is sound (other backpicks parse), so the gap is either the duplicate-identity tangle or that game's plays not loaded from the report-time crawl; worth a targeted check when that game is re-crawled.
- **Ethics:** coach-facing names the catcher/pitcher; player-facing stays tendency/number ("their catcher will throw behind you — get back hard, tighten your lead"), never derogatory.
